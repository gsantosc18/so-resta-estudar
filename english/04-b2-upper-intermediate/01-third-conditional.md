# Third Conditional (Terceira Condicional)

## Objetivo

Ao final deste tópico, o estudante será capaz de usar o *Third Conditional* para expressar arrependimentos, críticas e imaginar resultados diferentes para situações que já aconteceram no passado e não podem mais ser mudadas.

## Pré-requisitos

- [Past Perfect](../03-b1-intermediate/04-past-perfect.md)
- [Second Conditional](../03-b1-intermediate/06-second-conditional.md)
- [Modais de Passado (*would have, could have*)](../03-b1-intermediate/10-modal-verbs-might-should-would.md)

## Conceitos Fundamentais

### O que é o *Third Conditional*?

Se o *First Conditional* fala sobre o futuro possível, e o *Second Conditional* fala sobre o presente/futuro irreal, o **Third Conditional** é a condicional do **passado irreal**.

Nós o usamos para imaginar "o que teria acontecido se..." no passado. Como o passado não pode ser mudado, essas situações são 100% impossíveis. Frequentemente usamos essa estrutura para expressar **arrependimento** (*regret*) ou para fazer **críticas** ao comportamento passado.

Exemplo em português: *Se eu tivesse estudado mais, eu teria passado no exame.* (A realidade é que eu não estudei e não passei. Estou imaginando um passado alternativo).

### Formação

Estrutura: **If + Past Perfect , [Sujeito] + would have + Past Participle**

A estrutura é longa, mas lógica:
1. Na parte do *If*, usamos o *Past Perfect* (had + particípio).
2. Na parte do *Resultado*, usamos *Would have* + particípio (também chamado de *Perfect Conditional*).

| A Condição (Passado Irreal) | O Resultado (Passado Irreal) |
|-----------------------------|------------------------------|
| **If** I **had studied** harder, | I **would have passed** the exam. |
| **If** she **had left** earlier, | she **wouldn't have missed** the train. |
| **If** we **had known** about the party, | we **would have gone**. |

> **Vírgula**: Mantemos a regra das condicionais. Se a frase começar com *If*, usamos vírgula. Se a ordem for invertida, não há vírgula.
> *I would have passed the exam if I had studied harder.*

### Contrações

No inglês falado, a pronúncia do *Third Conditional* é muito contraída, o que dificulta o entendimento de quem está aprendendo.

- *had* vira **'d** (If I**'d** known...)
- *would have* vira **would've** (I **would've** gone...) ou até **woulda** em linguagem muito informal.

Frase falada naturalmente: *If I'd known, I would've told you.*

> **Atenção ao duplo 'd**:
> *If **I'd** known...* = If I **had** known (Passado Perfeito).
> *...**I'd** have told you.* = ...I **would** have told you (Condicional).

## Regras e Exceções

### Usando *Could have* e *Might have*

Podemos substituir o *would have* para alterar a certeza do resultado imaginado:

- **Would have**: Certeza (Eu teria feito).
  *If I had seen him, I **would have** said hello.*
- **Could have**: Capacidade/Possibilidade (Eu poderia ter feito / Teria tido a chance).
  *If you had asked me, I **could have** helped you.*
- **Might have**: Possibilidade fraca (Talvez eu tivesse feito).
  *If we had left earlier, we **might have** caught the train.*

### Omissão do *If* (Inversão)

No inglês formal (frequentemente escrito), podemos omitir a palavra *If* e inverter o sujeito com o auxiliar *had*. O significado é exatamente o mesmo.

- Normal: ***If I had known** about the problem, I would have helped.*
- Formal/Invertido: ***Had I known** about the problem, I would have helped.*

- Normal: ***If she had studied**, she would have passed.*
- Formal/Invertido: ***Had she studied**, she would have passed.*

## Erros Comuns

### 1. Usar *would* na frase do *If*

Este é o erro mais comum. O *would* NUNCA vai na cláusula com o *If*.

- ❌ *If I **would have known**, I would have gone.*
- ✅ *If I **had known**, I would have gone.*

### 2. Esquecer o *have* no resultado

Muitos alunos confundem com a *Second Conditional* e escrevem:

- ❌ *If I had seen her, I **would say** hello.* (Misturou 3ª com 2ª condicional).
- ✅ *If I had seen her, I **would have said** hello.*

### 3. Traduzir o português literalmente ("Se eu *tivesse*")

- ❌ *If I **have had** studied...* (Tradução literal e confusa).
- ✅ *If I **had** studied...* (Past Perfect).

## Exemplos

### Arrependimentos Pessoais

```
I was so tired yesterday. If I hadn't stayed up late watching Netflix, 
I wouldn't have been so sleepy at work. 

If I had accepted that job offer in London five years ago, my life 
would have been completely different.
```

### Críticas / Culpa

```
A: We are lost!
B: Well, if you had listened to me, we wouldn't have taken the wrong turn!
A: If you had brought the GPS like I asked, we would have arrived by now!
```

## Exercícios

### Nível 1 — Escolha a forma correta

1. If I (had seen / saw) you, I would have stopped to talk.
2. She would have passed the driving test if she (didn't make / hadn't made) that mistake.
3. If they had invited us, we (would go / would have gone).
4. What (would you do / would you have done) if you had found a million dollars on the street yesterday?
5. I (wouldn't have bought / didn't buy) the car if I had known it was broken.

### Nível 2 — Complete a frase com os verbos na Third Conditional

1. If you ______ (tell) me you were in town, I ______ (invite) you for dinner.
2. He ______ (not / have) the accident if he ______ (drive) more carefully.
3. If it ______ (not / rain) yesterday, we ______ (go) to the beach.
4. I ______ (could / finish) the project on time if the computer ______ (not / crash).

### Nível 3 — Transforme os "fatos do passado" em hipóteses (Third Conditional)

*Exemplo: He didn't study, so he failed the exam.*
*→ If he had studied, he wouldn't have failed the exam.*

1. I didn't set my alarm clock, so I woke up late.
   → If I ______, I ______.
2. We didn't have a map, so we got lost.
   → If we ______, we ______.
3. She didn't wear a coat, so she caught a cold.
   → If she ______, she ______.
